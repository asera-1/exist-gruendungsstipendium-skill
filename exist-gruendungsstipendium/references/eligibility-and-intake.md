# Eligibility and progressive intake

The criteria below are a **baseline snapshot**, researched from official sources on 2026-08-29. Revalidate them through `source-verification.md` before making a current eligibility claim.

## Contents

- Result labels
- Fast fit screen
- Progressive interview sequence
- Eligibility matrix

## Result labels

- **Likely compatible:** no known critical mismatch, subject to institutional and official review.
- **Unclear—confirm:** a decisive fact, interpretation, or exception requires evidence or confirmation.
- **Likely incompatible:** a current official criterion appears unmet; cite it and seek confirmation before ending the inquiry.

Never use `approved`, `guaranteed eligible`, or an approval probability.

## Fast fit screen

Ask only unanswered questions, starting with the highest-risk group.

### A. Institutional route

1. Which German university or research institution could submit the application?
2. Has its Gründungsnetzwerk or transfer office agreed to assess or support the project?
3. Is a Hochschullehrer/in or working-group leader who is employed by or on staff at the applicant institution available to be the one named mentor and project lead, and will that person sign the current declaration?
4. What workspace, laboratory, infrastructure, and administrative support are required?
5. Who owns relevant IP, and is access for the future company plausible?
6. Can and will the institution conclude the currently required stipend agreement with each proposed funded person, and who owns that process?

Baseline: the institution, not the founder, is the applicant and grant recipient. It must have its seat in Germany, sit within a qualifying Gründungsnetzwerk, provide institutional support and a mentor, administer funds, support commercialization and access to required IP, and conclude the required stipend agreements with funded team members. The 2026 handbook described the named mentor using the official categories Hochschullehrer/in or Arbeitsgruppenleiter/in at the applicant institution and assigned that person the project-lead role. Other advisers may contribute, but only one mentor is named in the application.

If the founder has no contact yet, use the current official EXIST application/network information and then the named institution's official startup, transfer, entrepreneurship, or technology-transfer pages to identify the responsible office. Report the office, page, and public contact route with an access date. Do not infer that the office has agreed to support the project, and do not send a message without explicit authorization.

### B. Team and academic status

For every proposed funded person, collect:

- intended role and weekly availability;
- intended status at project start: student, graduate, researcher/former researcher, doctorate holder, or vocationally qualified person;
- institution, qualification, subject, and exact completion date;
- exact end date of the last social-insurance employment at a university/research institution, if relevant;
- enrollment expected at project start, study format (including whether genuinely berufsbegleitend), prior completed degree, and student progress at project start;
- for a qualification from a non-EU state, whether the institution has retained the currently required recognition evidence; for another foreign qualification, whether any institutional equivalency check is required;
- contribution to the preparatory work and project know-how;
- current employment, stipend, paid side work, and company interests;
- previous EXIST funding, successful EXIST-Women participation, and comparable state innovation-startup funding, with program name, start/end, duration, sequence before/after the intended EGS period, and substantive venture overlap.

For a non-EU citizen, ask only the minimum formal question needed for screening: will a valid residence title cover the entire requested funding period at project start, and who at the institution has verified it? Record `verified yes/no`, the relevant coverage period, evidence holder, and unresolved action. Do not copy passports, residence-title numbers, scans, or unrelated nationality details into working drafts. The designated official form may require nationality and other personal data; enter those only when currently required and authorized.

Baseline checks:

- One to three stipend holders.
- Standard categories are students, graduates, and scientists/research employees. A completed degree or the relevant university/research employment normally falls within a five-year window.
- At most one person per team may use the exception for either a recognized vocational qualification **or** a degree/relevant scientific activity outside the normal five-year window; do not treat these as two separate exception slots.
- The current overview states that at least one person in the team must have a university degree.
- Students must have completed at least half of the required study work by project start. At the 2026 baseline, the handbook treated 50% of Bachelor's ECTS as sufficient and a Master's student with an already completed Bachelor's degree as satisfying the progress test.
- Classify status at project start, not at the date of the first conversation. Enrollment can affect the stipend category; a genuinely berufsbegleitend course was treated differently in the 2026 handbook. Where its compact wording or an unusual enrollment pattern is unclear, obtain institutional/PtJ confirmation.
- Proposed founders should be the know-how holders who completed material preparatory work.

Use this team-composition screen after refreshing the current official material:

| Proposed funded team | Baseline treatment | Reason/action |
|---|---|---|
| One eligible recent graduate, doctorate holder, or qualifying recent scientist/research employee who also holds a university degree | Standard route, subject to all other checks | Verify exact degree/employment dates and project-start status |
| One student, one vocationally qualified person, or one person relying only on the over-five-year exception | **Likely incompatible under the 2026 handbook; official-source conflict must be surfaced** | The overview requires at least one university degree and the handbook's solo examples exclude these constellations; if a student exception is argued from the guideline, obtain a written case-specific PtJ/network answer |
| Two or three people with no exception and at least one university graduate | Standard composition route | Check each person's dates, enrollment, work and know-how contribution |
| Team using one vocationally qualified **or** one over-five-year person | Possible single exception | Confirm there is no second exception and at least one qualifying university graduate |
| Team majority-student at project start | **Unclear—case-specific exception** | The guideline and handbook allow only a reasoned individual review; do not infer approval from innovation quality alone |
| Team using two exception persons | **Likely incompatible** | Current official material allows one exception per funded team |

Previous funding is not automatically disqualifying, but different programs require different tests. The 2026 handbook allowed repeat EXIST funding when the ventures are separated in content and time. It described successful EXIST-Women participation as scientific activity that can offset a university degree older than five years. Comparable state funding must instead be checked under its program-specific before/after sequencing rule; at the baseline, qualifying state support before EGS was limited to 12 months. Record program, dates, duration, sequence, funded person and venture overlap separately. Never extend the repeat-EXIST rule to another program by analogy.

### C. Timing, company, work, and other support

Ask:

1. What is the intended submission date, expected date of a complete application, and project start? Does the start respect the current minimum interval after receipt of a complete application, the outer application date, the institution's lead time, and the current standard project duration?
2. Has any UG, GmbH, AG, or other capital company been formed for this project? Give dates and purpose.
3. Has business activity begun in any form—sales, invoices, paid pilots, contracts, commercial launch, or commissioned development?
4. Does any team member hold management roles or shares in another company?
5. What exact employment relationship, stipend, public support, or paid side work will overlap the intended funding period, and for how many hours per month?
6. Is any product development commissioned by or mainly serving a third party?

Baseline checks:

- On 2026-08-29, the binding guideline allowed applications through 2027-12-31 and described project start as possible no earlier than three months after receipt of a complete application. The current handbook described a fixed standard project period of 12 months. Refresh all three facts; `rolling/no fixed rounds` does not remove the outer date, completeness gate, minimum lead time, or the institution's earlier internal deadline.
- The handbook described a narrow one-time extension of up to three months for the specified childbirth/parental-support case. Treat it as a conditional exception, not ordinary planning capacity, and verify the current requirements before relying on it.
- A capital company for the project and/or its business activity must not have started by project start. Formation and activity during funding may be possible under separation requirements.
- Another stipend or program funding the person's living costs cannot run in parallel; a dormant employment relationship may be possible.
- **Employment conflict:** the binding guideline excludes a simultaneous active employment relationship and separately excludes paid side work above 20 hours per month. The 2026 handbook's operational checklist grouped employment and paid side work together as permissible below 20 hours per month. Do not resolve this from wording alone. Record employment type, active/dormant status, monthly hours and dates, then return `unclear—confirm` unless the current institution/PtJ interpretation clearly covers the case.
- Development commissioned by or directly serving a third party is excluded.

Existing sole-trader/freelance activity, pre-existing companies, prior revenue, paid pilots, and unusual corporate interests always require a fact-specific institutional/PtJ confirmation.

### D. International status and submission language

Ask only when relevant:

1. For a non-EU citizen, has the institution verified a residence title covering the full requested funding period at project start?
2. For a qualification from a non-EU state, has the institution retained the currently required recognition evidence? For another foreign qualification, does the institution require an equivalency check?
3. Is there a German-speaking team member, and which language does the current institution/PtJ instruction permit for the Ideenpapier?

At the 2026 baseline, the handbook allowed an English Ideenpapier when no German-speaking team member was present. Do not generalize that sentence to all English submissions; verify the current rule. Certificates, birth records and residence evidence were not routinely uploaded under the current handbook, application page and generator, but were retained by the institution for possible review. Keep personal evidence out of shared drafting material.

### E. Idea and commercialization

Ask the founders to explain, with evidence where available:

- the specific customer and painful problem;
- the product, technology, or knowledge-based service;
- what is materially new compared with direct competitors, indirect alternatives, and the status quo;
- current development state and feasibility evidence;
- the university/research origin, prior project, knowledge, or support link;
- why the intended future company will develop and commercialize it itself;
- customer benefit and early validation;
- business model, price logic, market-entry segment, and path to viability;
- contribution to relevant sustainability objectives;
- IP ownership and protection/access strategy.

Baseline checks:

- The project is an ambitious innovative university/research-related venture, not a minor modification or combination without significant distinction.
- It addresses a defined customer problem, creates clear customer benefit, and differs materially from competitive solutions.
- It supports technical or organizational change, shows a plausible route to economic viability, and addresses sustainability.
- The innovation is intended to be implemented and commercialized in the founders’ own future company.
- Traditional liberal-profession practice is excluded under the baseline guideline.

An idea that originated entirely outside the institution but will be supported by it is **unclear—confirm** unless current official sources and the institution resolve the case.

## Progressive interview sequence

Do not present all fields at once.

1. **Institution and timing:** applicant institution/network, intended submission, start and duration, company/activity status.
2. **Team:** composition, each person's project-start category, dates, enrollment, availability, prior funding and overlapping support.
3. **Conditional formal status:** residence-title coverage, foreign-qualification recognition, and submission language only where relevant.
4. **Innovation fit:** problem, solution, novelty, current state, university link, commercialization.
5. **Delivery readiness:** roles, named mentor/project lead, IP, infrastructure, milestones, customers, market entry.
6. **Application evidence:** documents, calculations, LOIs, research results, financial assumptions, and current local forms.

After each batch, update the matrix and ask only questions that can change the conclusion or unlock the next section.
